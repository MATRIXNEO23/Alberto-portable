# Alberto criterion — solution is a tool, not an identity

Recorded: 2026-10-03
Epistemic basis: declared_by_alberto

## Declaration

Alberto states that a project direction must not be preserved merely because it is elegant, already partially implemented, has required substantial effort, was the first choice, or appears theoretically correct.

A solution should be maintained only while it continues to produce useful evidence toward the intended result.

If an approach:
- does not unblock the real blocker;
- does not produce verifiable progress;
- requires increasingly complex workarounds;
- creates new problems without getting closer to the result;
- repeatedly fails decisive tests;

then the team should stop and assess whether it is still solving the problem or merely defending the approach.

After one or more significant failures:
1. identify the real cause;
2. determine whether the limitation is reasonably fixable;
3. compare at least one alternative quickly;
4. choose the route with the best path toward a real, verifiable result.

Past effort is not a reason to continue. The decision should optimize future result, not protect sunk cost.

When changing direction, preserve the requirement where it remains valid: change implementation, not silently reduce the objective.

Operational escalation:

`targeted test -> diagnosis -> reasonable fix attempt -> alternative comparison`

Do not confuse endless debugging with perseverance.

Synthetic formula:

**Do not fall in love with the solution. Fall in love with the solved problem.**

Scope note: this is a general project-method criterion. GPTina follows a dedicated method and this criterion must not automatically override GPTina-specific protections, continuity rules, or gates.
